## Why you should always review

You're responsible for what you bring into the classroom, even if a machine wrote it. UNESCO insists that teachers must always keep the role of **validating** AI-generated content.

<Callout type="cuidado" title="Golden rule">
**If you can't verify it, don't use it.**
</Callout>

## Checklist

You can print this page and use it every time you review a material.

### 1. Accuracy

- [ ] The facts, dates, formulas and definitions are correct.
- [ ] I checked the information against a reliable source (textbook, official website, academic article).
- [ ] The references or quotes that appear really exist.

### 2. Curricular relevance

- [ ] The material serves **my learning objective**.
- [ ] It's consistent with the Ministry of Education's (MEN) Basic Competency Standards and Basic Learning Rights (DBA) for the grade.
- [ ] The activities get students to **think and do**, not just copy.

### 3. Appropriate level

- [ ] The vocabulary, length and complexity are right for the age group.
- [ ] It offers **just-right scaffolding**: not so easy that it bores, not so hard that it frustrates.

### 4. Real adaptation

- [ ] Each version changes something specific (content, process or product) for a specific reason (readiness, interests or learning profile).
- [ ] All the versions aim at **the same objective**.

### 5. Inclusive, unbiased language

- [ ] There are no stereotypes about gender, region, ethnicity, disability or economic status.
- [ ] The names, characters and situations reflect diversity.

### 6. Local context

- [ ] The examples make sense to my students (Colombian pesos, places, customs and dates from our calendar).
- [ ] There are no references from other countries that they won't understand, such as dollars, snowy seasons or holidays they don't celebrate.

### 7. Accessibility (UDL)

- [ ] The information is available in more than one format (text, image, audio or diagram).
- [ ] The instructions are short and clear, and the font is easy to read.
- [ ] The images have a description in words.

### 8. Respect and safety

- [ ] It contains no personal data about students.
- [ ] The images and texts are age-appropriate.
- [ ] If I used texts or ideas from other authors, I cited them.

## Warning signs: when AI makes things up

Be suspicious and check more carefully when you see:

- **Quotes or references** you can't find when you search for them.
- **Very precise figures with no source**: exact percentages, numbers or dates.
- **Answers that change** if you ask the same question again.
- **Claims that contradict** what you know about your subject.
- **Images with errors**: garbled text, strange hands, wrong maps or flags.

## What do I do if I find errors?

- **A small error:** fix it yourself.
- **A recurring error:** ask the AI to fix it and explain what's wrong.
- **Lots of errors:** rewrite the prompt with more context, give the AI the reference text ("base your answer only on this document") or use a tool that works with your own sources, such as NotebookLM.

<Resumen>
- **Always** review a material before taking it into the classroom: the responsibility is yours.
- Use the checklist: **accuracy, relevance, level, adaptation, bias, context, accessibility and respect**.
- Learn to recognize the **signs that the AI made up** information.
</Resumen>
