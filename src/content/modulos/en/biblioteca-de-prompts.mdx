## How to use this library

1. **Choose** the template that looks most like what you need.
2. **Copy** the text and **replace** everything in `[square brackets]` with the details of your class.
3. **Review** the result with the checklist from Step 6 and **adjust** it by continuing the conversation with the AI.

Each template shows **what it adapts** (content, process or product) and **based on what** (readiness, interests or learning profile), so you can connect each example with what you saw in Step 2.

<Callout type="cuidado">
Remember: in the brackets, describe the **group** or its **needs**, never a student's personal data.
</Callout>

## To get started: let the AI ask you

**Adapts:** any material · **Ideal for:** when you don't know where to start.

<PromptExample label="Template">
Act as an instructional coach who is an expert in differentiated instruction.
I want to create a [subject] material for grade [grade] students about [topic].
Before you create it, ask me, one at a time, any questions you need about my students, my objective and my context. When you have enough information, propose the material and briefly explain why you designed it that way.
</PromptExample>

## 1. A reading at three levels

**Adapts:** content · **Based on:** readiness · **Ideal for:** any subject.

<PromptExample label="Template">
Act as a [subject] teacher with experience in differentiated instruction.
My students are in grade [grade] ([age] years old) and have different reading levels.
Learning objective: [objective].
Write a text about [topic] in three versions that teach the same key ideas:
- Level 1 (more support): short sentences, simple vocabulary and a 5-word glossary.
- Level 2 (expected): about [number] words, with grade-level vocabulary.
- Level 3 (challenge): more depth, a fun fact and a reflection question.

At the end of each version, include 3 comprehension questions. Use examples from the Colombian context.
</PromptExample>

**Example of use:** social studies, grade 6, "the natural regions of Colombia".

## 2. Graded exercises with hints

**Adapts:** process · **Based on:** readiness · **Ideal for:** math and science.

<PromptExample label="Template">
You are a grade [grade] [subject] teacher.
Objective: [objective].
Create [number] exercises about [topic], ordered from easiest to hardest, in three blocks:
- Block A: with a step-by-step worked example before starting.
- Block B: similar exercises, each with an optional hint.
- Block C: an everyday problem that requires explaining the reasoning.

Put the hints at the end, separate from the exercises, so students can decide whether to use them. Include the answers in a separate section for the teacher.
</PromptExample>

**Example of use:** math, grade 7, "operations with integers".

## 3. An explanation based on interests

**Adapts:** content · **Based on:** interests · **Ideal for:** abstract or less motivating topics.

<PromptExample label="Template">
Explain [topic] to grade [grade] students using examples from [interest 1], [interest 2] and [interest 3].
Write a short explanation (at most [number] words) for each interest. All three must teach exactly the same concept and end with the same check-for-understanding question.
</PromptExample>

**Example of use:** physics, grade 9, "speed", with examples from soccer, cycling and video games.

## 4. Graphic organizer

**Adapts:** content · **Based on:** learning profile · **Ideal for:** synthesis and review.

<PromptExample label="Template">
Using the following text, create a [concept map / comparison chart / timeline] for grade [grade] students.
Use at most [number] concepts, with clear linking words. Present it first as a hierarchical list that I can turn into a drawing and then as a description in words for anyone who can't see the diagram.
Base it only on the text:
[paste the text here]
</PromptExample>

**Example of use:** natural science, grade 8, "animal cells and plant cells" in a comparison chart.

## 5. Quiz with feedback

**Adapts:** process · **Based on:** readiness · **Ideal for:** formative assessment.

<PromptExample label="Template">
Create a [number]-question multiple-choice quiz about [topic] for grade [grade].
Objective it assesses: [objective].
For each question, include four options, the correct answer and short feedback for each wrong option that explains the student's most likely mistake.
Order the questions from simplest to most complex.
</PromptExample>

**Example of use:** language arts, grade 4, "nouns, adjectives and verbs".

## 6. Assessment rubric

**Adapts:** product · **Ideal for:** projects, presentations and written work.

<PromptExample label="Template">
Create a rubric to assess [product] by grade [grade] students.
Learning objective: [objective].
Use [number] criteria and four performance levels: superior, high, basic and low.
Write each descriptor in language students can understand, phrased positively, describing what the student achieves. Present it in a table.
</PromptExample>

**Example of use:** social studies, grade 10, "an oral presentation about an environmental problem in the neighborhood".

<Callout type="consejo">
Check that the performance levels match your institution's grading scale (for example, Colombia's national scale: superior, high, basic and low).
</Callout>

## 7. A version in multiple formats (UDL)

**Adapts:** content and product · **Based on:** learning profile · **Ideal for:** diverse groups.

<PromptExample label="Template">
I have this material for grade [grade] about [topic]:
[paste the material here]

Following Universal Design for Learning, create:
1. An easy-to-read version, with short sentences and one idea per paragraph.
2. An audio script of 2 minutes at most that explains the same thing.
3. A proposed visual diagram, described in words.
4. Three options for students to show what they learned (for example: written, oral or graphic).
</PromptExample>

**Example of use:** any study guide you already have and want to make more accessible.

## 8. A preschool activity

**Adapts:** process · **Based on:** learning profile · **Ideal for:** early childhood education.

<PromptExample label="Template">
Act as an early childhood teacher.
Design a [length]-minute activity for [age]-year-old children about [topic].
It must include: a time for play, a short song or rhyme, an activity with hands-on materials that are easy to find in Colombia and a closing question to talk about.
Write instructions I can read aloud. Keep in mind that the children can't read yet.
</PromptExample>

**Example of use:** kindergarten (transición), age 5, "the colors and fruits of my region".

## 9. An ethical dilemma for discussion

**Adapts:** process · **Based on:** interests · **Ideal for:** philosophy, ethics and social studies.

<PromptExample label="Template">
Create a short ethical dilemma (150 words at most) for grade [grade] students about [topic], set in an everyday situation for young people in Colombia.
Include: the situation, two possible decisions with arguments for each one, and five questions for a group debate that move from the concrete to the philosophical.
Don't say which answer is correct.
</PromptExample>

**Example of use:** philosophy, grade 11, "honesty and using AI for homework".

## 10. Support for reading difficulties

**Adapts:** content · **Based on:** learning profile · **Ideal for:** students who need reading supports.

<PromptExample label="Template">
Adapt the following text for a grade [grade] student who needs reading supports.
- Use short sentences (15 words at most) and common vocabulary.
- Split the text into short blocks, each with a subheading.
- Put the key words in bold and explain them at the end.
- Keep all the important ideas of the original text.

Text:
[paste the text here]
</PromptExample>

**Example of use:** any class reading that a student needs in an easy-to-read format.

## 11. A menu of options for the final product

**Adapts:** product · **Based on:** interests and learning profile · **Ideal for:** wrapping up a unit.

<PromptExample label="Template">
To wrap up a unit about [topic] in grade [grade], propose a menu of 6 final product options that let students show the same learning: [objective].
Include written, oral, visual and hands-on options. For each option, give a description, the (low-cost) materials needed and the estimated time.
Add 3 assessment criteria shared by all the options.
</PromptExample>

**Example of use:** natural science, grade 5, "the ecosystems of my region".

<Resumen>
- The templates are a **starting point**: change them, combine them and improve them.
- Replace the brackets with descriptions of your **group**, never with personal data.
- Save the versions that work for you in your **prompt bank**, and always review the result.
</Resumen>
