## Start with the right question

The question isn't "which AI is the best?", but: **what material do I need to create, and under what conditions will I be working?** The right tool depends on your goal, your resources and who is going to use it.

## Six criteria for deciding

1. **Type of material.** Do you need text, images, a presentation or audio? Not all tools generate images.
2. **Cost.** Almost all of them have a free version with limits (number of messages or available features). Start with the free one and decide later.
3. **Quality in your language.** Test it with a topic you know well. Check the spelling, how natural the language sounds and whether it uses expressions from your context.
4. **Privacy.** Does it use your conversations to train its models? Can you turn that off in the settings? Never enter your students' personal data (you'll see this in Step 7).
5. **Minimum age.** If your students are going to use it directly, check the permitted age. UNESCO recommends that generative AI not be used in the classroom before age 13.
6. **Institutional access.** If your school or university uses Google Workspace or Microsoft 365, you may already have access to Gemini or Copilot with your institutional account, sometimes with better data protection.

## The best-known tools

These four tools are general-purpose: they work for almost any subject and level.

| Tool | Company | Minimum age* | Useful for | Keep in mind |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **ChatGPT** | OpenAI | 13, with an adult's permission if under 18 | Text, activity ideas, images and file analysis. | It's the most popular. The free version has usage limits. |
| **Gemini** | Google | 13 (in some countries, minors with a supervised account) | Text and images. Integrates with Docs, Slides and Classroom. | Convenient if your institution uses Google Workspace. |
| **Copilot** | Microsoft | 13 | Text and images. Integrates with Word and PowerPoint. | Convenient if your institution uses Microsoft 365. |
| **Claude** | Anthropic | 18 | Long texts, document analysis and simple interactive materials. | For teacher use only: it doesn't allow accounts for minors. |

\* According to each service's general terms, checked in October 2026. Conditions change often and may vary by country: **always check them on the official page** before asking your students to use a tool.

## Other useful tools

- **NotebookLM (Google).** It works only with the documents you upload: your study guides, the textbook or Colombia's Basic Learning Rights (DBA). This reduces the risk of made-up information, and it creates summaries, study guides and audio overviews.
- **AI design tools** (such as Canva). Useful for giving a visual format to worksheets, posters and infographics.
- **AI presentation generators.** They create slides from a text. Always check the content and the images they suggest.

<Callout type="consejo">
Choose **one or two tools** and learn to use them well. Knowing how to ask (Step 4) matters more than the tool: the same principles work in all of them.
</Callout>

## A quick test before adopting a tool

Ask for the same material in two different tools and compare the results with these questions:

- Is the content **correct**?
- Is the language **right for my students' age**?
- Did it follow **all my instructions** (format, length, level)?
- **How much did I have to correct** to get it ready?

<Callout type="cuidado">
Tools are updated almost every month: their features, limits and paid plans change. What's free today may not be tomorrow. That's why this guide focuses on **criteria** that don't go out of date, rather than on buttons or menus.
</Callout>

<Resumen>
- Choose the tool based on **the material you need and your context**, not on what's trendy.
- Check **cost, quality in your language, privacy, minimum age and institutional access**.
- Master one or two tools: **knowing how to ask** is what makes the biggest difference.
</Resumen>
