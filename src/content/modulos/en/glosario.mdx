**Adaptive learning.** A learning experience that adjusts to each student's needs, pace and preferences, instead of offering a single piece of content that's the same for everyone.

**Adaptive teaching material.** A resource designed with pedagogical intent to fit students' characteristics, pace, interests and needs. With AI, teachers can generate, personalize and refine it through prompts.

**Artificial intelligence (AI).** A computer system that, from the information it receives, infers how to produce outputs such as predictions, recommendations, decisions or content (OECD, 2024). Generative AI is one type of it.

**Basic Learning Rights (DBA).** The knowledge and skills that, according to Colombia's Ministry of Education (MEN), students should learn in each grade and subject. In Spanish, *Derechos Básicos de Aprendizaje*. They're a reference point for checking a material's curricular relevance.

**Bias.** AI's tendency to reproduce prejudices or stereotypes present in the data it learned from, for example about gender, region, ethnicity or economic status.

**Differentiated instruction.** An approach that deliberately adjusts the content, the process or the product according to students' readiness, interests or learning profile.

**Generative AI (GenAI).** A branch of artificial intelligence that can create new content (text, images, audio, video or code) from the user's instructions.

**Hallucination.** When AI generates false information that sounds true: facts, quotes or references that don't exist. That's why everything must be checked.

**Knowledge cutoff.** The point in time up to which the data used to train an AI model goes. It may have no information about what happened after that, unless it searches the internet.

**Language model.** An AI system trained on enormous amounts of text to understand and produce language. It's the "engine" behind tools like ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot or Claude.

**Machine learning.** The way most AI systems learn: instead of following rules written step by step, they find patterns in many examples and use them on new cases.

**Multimodal.** An AI tool that can take in or produce several kinds of information at once: text, images, audio or documents.

**Prompt.** An instruction given to a generative AI tool to guide the content it will produce. It's the link between your pedagogical intent and what the tool can do.

**Prompt bank.** A personal document where you save the prompts that worked for you, organized by topic and grade, so you can reuse and improve them.

**Prompt engineering.** The skill of designing, adjusting and improving the instructions given to AI to get relevant, accurate and useful results.

**Scaffolding.** The temporary supports given to a student so they can achieve something they can't yet do alone: hints, worked examples, guiding questions. They're removed as the student becomes more independent.

**Teacher digital competence.** The knowledge, skills and attitudes that let teachers use digital technologies critically, ethically and with a sound pedagogical basis to teach better.

**Teaching material.** A resource ready to use in class that carries the teacher's learning objectives inside it: a reading, a study guide, a workshop, a presentation or an assessment.

**Universal Design for Learning (UDL).** A framework that proposes designing flexible materials and lessons from the start, with multiple means of engagement, representation, and action and expression, so that everyone can learn. In Spanish, *Diseño Universal para el Aprendizaje* (DUA).

**Zone of proximal development.** Vygotsky's concept: the distance between what a student can do alone and what they can do with the help of someone more expert. A good material sits in that zone.
